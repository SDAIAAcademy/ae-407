The capstone has six hours: 20 minutes of brief, 170 of building, 20 of final preparation, 140 of defences, which is seven defences per panel, and 10 of review and closing. With more participants, run panels side by side; do not shorten the 20 minutes.

The panel reads logs, not summaries. A rate needs its rows, with timestamps and the target version.

The repository is 60% of the course mark and the live defence 20%; the supervised check made up the other 20%. Passing needs 70% overall and at least 60% on this rubric. Full marks on testing and findings: three findings, each from ten or more trials in a stated script, each with a regression test that fails on the build as found and passes with the fix.

Format

Ten minutes of briefing, ten minutes of questions

1 · The page

Brief the executive from your statement: what remains, your recommendation, the conditions with owners and dates.

2 · One finding, live

The auditor picks a claim. You reproduce the number behind it from the repository.

3 · The supplier's notice

The model changes. You rerun your regression tests on the new version and say whether the statement still holds.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes the one condition they would add.

Questions the panel asks everyone: which of your residual scores are true today and which are promises? Which of your zeros come from a control outside the model? What did you not test? Who owns the condition due first? The notice is handed over about three minutes into the questions; the command is in the capstone README.
